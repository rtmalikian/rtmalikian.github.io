REVIEW 1 · 22 OCT 2026AMBIENT AI SCRIBESSUBMITTED — J CLINICAL EPIDEMIOLOGY

Ambient AI scribes for clinical documentation: documentation time, after-hours work, clinician burnout, note quality and note errors

1,049 records found · 567 screened after deduplication · 74 studies included

Scribes cut documentation time by about a fifth — and 59.8% of audited AI-drafted notes contained at least one error. The time saving and the checking burden are the same intervention.
−18.9% documentation timePooled percentage change, 15 studies · GRADE low
SMD −0.44 work exhaustion2 randomised trials, 30–90 days · GRADE moderate for the trial estimate
18.0% omissions · 11.5% hallucinationsOne audit of 356 notes against the consultation record · GRADE very low

REVIEW 2 · 22 OCT 2026 · ROBUSTNESS PASS 23 OCTLLM CITATION FABRICATIONRESEARCH INTEGRITY

Fabricated and inaccurate citations generated by large language models for biomedical topics

16,474 records screened · 63 generation experiments · 148 model arms · 22,461 references checked one by one

About 1 in 5 LLM-generated biomedical references did not exist at all — 19.4% completely fabricated, only 37.0% fully accurate. Turning retrieval on cut fabrication to 5.8%, versus 31.0% with retrieval off.

REVIEW 3 · 31 OCT 2026LLM CLINICAL SUMMARIESREPORTING GAP FINDING

Clinically significant errors in LLM-generated clinical summaries: omissions, inaccuracies and fabrications per summary

991 records found · 483 screened after deduplication · 11 studies included · 22 arms · All summary types, discharge as subgroup

About 3 in 5 LLM summaries contained at least one error of any audited type — yet only 1 of 11 studies reported clinically significant errors per summary. In that one study, 21.0% of summaries carried a critical, treatment-impacting error. The literature rarely counts the error that matters most.
60.6% any errorSupplementary pool, 5 units, 323 summaries · GRADE very low
20.7% omissions · 27.9% inaccuraciesPooled per-summary proportions · GRADE very low
17.6% fabrication / additionRange 0–97.5% across studies — the spread is the finding · GRADE very low
Primary outcome: not pooledOnly 1 study reported it per summary — a formal reporting-gap result

HOW THEY WERE BUILT

The method is part of the claim — so the method is now open source too. A multi-agent team, strict independence rules, and a shipping gate that failed builds do not pass.

👥 A team, not a prompt

Information specialist, dual screeners who never see each other’s calls, adjudicators who settle disagreements by re-reading, dual extractors, a risk-of-bias pair, statistician, GRADE assessor, methods editor and a separate visual-QA pass.

🔍 Nothing invented, nothing hidden

No invented numbers, counts or citations. A source that was not run is never reported as searched. Blocked sources, discrepancies and corrections stay visible in the team log.

📊 Checked two ways

Random-effects models named in advance, heterogeneity and prediction intervals reported, at least one pool hand-checked in full, and code + data published so anyone can rebuild every figure.

🖼️ Figures inspected, not assumed

Every forest plot carries a summary diamond. Every figure and table page is rendered and visually inspected before shipping. A build that fails inspection does not ship.
